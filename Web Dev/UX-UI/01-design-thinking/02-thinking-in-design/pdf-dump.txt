UX & UI
Digital experiences
Digital Experience (DX) refers to the overall experience a user has when
interacting with a digital product or service across multiple touchpoints,
including websites, mobile apps, and other digital platforms
It encompasses both the User Interface (UI) and the User Experience (UX)
focusing on providing a seamless and satisfying journey for users
Digital Experience
4
The overall user interaction with
digital services or products across
platforms like websites and apps
Digital Experience
User Interface (UI) emphasizes the visual and interactive elements of a digital
product or service
It includes design elements such as layout, typography, colors, icons, and
imagery, as well as interactive components like buttons, forms, menus, and
navigation controls
The primary goal of UI design is to create a visually appealing and intuitive
interface that guides users through the product or service and facilitates their
interaction with it
User Interface
6
The design of visual elements and
flows that facilitate user interaction
with software, websites, or devices
User Interface
User Experience (UX) specifically emphasizes the design and usability aspects of
the digital experience, ensuring that real human users can easily accomplish
their tasks and goals with minimal friction or confusion
It involves understanding user behaviors, preferences, and needs to create
intuitive and enjoyable interactions
It encompasses design, usability, and overall satisfaction with the user interface
and interactions
User Experience
8
Enhancing satisfaction by improving
usability, accessibility, and enjoyment
in user interactions
User Experience
Design thinking process
A method for problem-solving that emphasizes understanding people's needs,
technological possibilities, and business viability
It's iterative, meaning it involves cycles of creating, testing, and refining solutions
Design Thinking

Design Thinking
Creative problem-solving that
emphasizes user needs, ideation,
prototyping, and iterative testing
1. Empathize
Understand needs and experiences of those you design for
2. Define
Clearly define problem based on what you've learned
3. Ideate
Generate diverse solutions to inspire creativity & innovation
Design Thinking
13
4. Prototype
Create tangible representations of your ideas to test and refine
5. Test
Test prototypes, gather user feedback, iterate for improvements
6. Implement
Integrate solution into business strategy, ensuring feasibility

User research
User research
User research aims to understand users' needs, behaviors, and preferences to
design products or services that meet their requirements effectively
● Conducting interviews
● Observing users
● Analyzing existing data
16

Conversations with users to gather

points, and expectations
User observation
Watching users using products or
doing tasks to understand behaviors
and usability issues
Analyzing Existing Data
Review quantitative data like user

trends, and areas to improve
Definition & ideation
Personas are created to represent a category or type of user behavior
They are highly useful for communicating collected information and insights
during research and for organizing our understanding of the research data
When little is known about the audience for a product or service, the following
aspects are encompassed by personas: Goals, Motivations, Needs, Expectations,
Challenges, Technology Considerations, and Accessibility
Personas

It is necessary to collect existing information and data because there is often

It's important to collect both qualitative data like customer service call logs and

Brainstorming
Generating multiple ideas within a
time limit, welcoming wild
suggestions, to solve problems
Brainstorming is aimed at generating ideas efficiently and producing a multitude
of ideas within a set time constraint, prioritizing quantity over quality, and
encouraging unconventional ideas
Conducted in teams but individually, brainstorming sessions focus on generating
ideas for features, services, or other elements beneficial to users and the
Following the session, the best ideas are selected for further development in
subsequent phases
Brainstorming
27
Information architecture
Organizing content based on user
mental models, expectations and goals
through the use of a sitemap
Information architecture (IA) is the structural design of digital information
environments, such as websites, apps, and software, to facilitate navigation,
findability, and usability
It involves creating a visual sitemap by organizing and categorizing content in a
hierarchical organization of content and navigation pathways that align with
users' mental models and makes it easy for them to locate and access
information
Information architecture
29
User flow
A visual representation illustrating
steps users take to accomplish tasks
in digital products, for optimization
Illustrates the path users follow as they interact with various screens, features,
and functionalities, guiding them from entry points to desired outcomes
User flows help designers understand and optimize the user journey by
identifying key touchpoints, decision points, and potential pain points,
ultimately contributing to the creation of more intuitive and user-friendly digital
experiences
User flow
31
Prototyping
Wireframing
Sketching the layout of a webpage or
app to show structure, functionality,
and UX before development
Wireframing is a critical phase in interface design involving creating low-fidelity
prototypes, like handmade or digital wireframes, to visualize the interface's main
structure
These wireframes focus on elements such as spacing, interaction zones, and
flows, providing a foundation for exploring and iterating on design solutions
before moving into detailed visual design phases
Wireframing
34
Mockups
Visual drafts showcasing design
elements, aiding communication,
feedback, and development
Low & high-fidelity
Mockups range from low to high
fidelity, spanning simple drafts to
detailed, realistic representations
Visual representations or drafts of a design idea, often created to illustrate the
layout, functionality, and visual aspects of a product or interface
They typically depict key elements such as user interface components, content
placement, color schemes, typography, and overall aesthetics
Mockups can be low or high-fidelity designs and are used to communicate and
validate design concepts, gather feedback from stakeholders, and guide the
development of the final product
Mockups
37
Testing
Usability testing
Evaluating product effectiveness and
efficiency by observing real users,
getting feedback, and refining the UX
Usability testing assesses product or system effectiveness and efficiency by
observing real users interacting with it, presenting tasks resembling typical use
cases, and gathering feedback on their experience, including ease of use,
intuitiveness, efficiency, and satisfaction
It identifies usability issues, enabling iterative refinement and enhancement of
the user interface and experience
Usability testing
40
Iteration
After usability testing, the design process typically involves iterative cycles
where prototypes are refined based on user feedback
These cycles include making adjustments and improvements to address
identified issues, followed by re-testing to evaluate the effectiveness of the
changes
This iterative approach continues until the prototypes meet the desired level of
usability and effectiveness
Iteration
42
Implementation
Final implementation in design thinking follows testing, where prototypes are
translated into products or services through collaboration between design and
development teams
It involves creating specifications, coding, interface building, feature integration,
and quality assurance testing
The design process doesn't end after implementation. Post-launch, iterative
improvements based on feedback and usage data analysis are common
Implementation
